# Contributing to Sports Intelligence

Thank you for your interest in Sports Intelligence. This project grows through shared curiosity, discussion and collaborative exploration.

You do not need academic credentials, specialist knowledge, coding experience or a GitHub account to contribute to a discussion. Different backgrounds and perspectives are welcome.

## Ways to contribute

### 1. Suggest a question or topic

Open a GitHub Issue and describe:
- What are you curious about?
- Which sport, league, country, audience or industry does it involve?
- Why might the question be interesting?
- What would you like the community to explore?

A question does not need to be fully developed before you share it.

### 2. Share an observation or experience

You can contribute:
- Personal experiences as a fan or spectator
- Observations from attending a sporting event
- Comparisons between countries, leagues or sports
- Cultural or social interpretations
- Questions or counterarguments

Please make clear when something is personal experience or interpretation rather than verified information.

### 3. Share useful sources

Useful material may include:
- Academic papers and books
- Official league, team or event information
- Public datasets and statistics
- Industry reports and financial information
- Journalism and interviews
- Photos, videos or social media examples, where sharing is lawful

Include a link and enough context for others to understand why the source is relevant. Do not upload material you do not have permission to redistribute.

### 4. Help develop research outputs

If a discussion develops, contributors may help create:
- Case studies
- Research briefs
- Literature summaries
- Data analyses and visualisations
- Comparative reports
- Articles or presentations

Contributions can be small. You may help with a question, a source, a translation, a data point or a critical review.

### 5. Contribute to the repository

If you are comfortable with GitHub, you can:
- Edit or improve a Markdown document
- Add a sourced case example
- Correct an error
- Submit a pull request
- Suggest a new research framework or dataset

Coding is optional. Please explain the purpose of substantial changes in your pull request.

## A simple discussion structure

When exploring a topic, these prompts can help:

1. **Question:** What are we trying to understand?
2. **Context:** Which sport, place, audience or period are we discussing?
3. **Observations:** What have people noticed or experienced?
4. **Evidence:** What sources or data might help us investigate?
5. **Perspectives:** What other explanations or viewpoints should we consider?
6. **Next steps:** What could we investigate or document next?

This is a guide, not a mandatory template.

## Good research practice

- Link to original sources whenever possible.
- Check dates, definitions and the context behind statistics.
- Avoid presenting correlation as causation.
- Distinguish facts, interpretations, hypotheses and personal opinions.
- Be transparent about limitations and uncertainty.
- Respect privacy and obtain consent before sharing identifiable personal information.
- Credit the work and ideas of others.

## Communication

Be constructive, curious and respectful. Disagreement is welcome when it engages with ideas rather than attacking people.

For expectations around respectful participation, see the [Code of Conduct](CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md).

Thank you for helping make sports a starting point for wider inquiry.
