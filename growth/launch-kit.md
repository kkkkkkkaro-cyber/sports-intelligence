# Sports Intelligence — Growth & Community Launch Kit

This document is an internal, practical guide for recruiting the first active contributors. The first audience is the existing sports-interest group: people who can share observations, questions, examples, sources and perspectives. Participation does not require coding or a GitHub account.

## 1. First growth objective

Recruit 10 genuinely active contributors before optimising for stars or raw traffic.

A useful early contributor is someone who does at least one of the following:
- Shares a first-hand sports or spectator experience.
- Suggests a researchable question.
- Shares a credible article, dataset, report or academic source.
- Offers a comparison across sports, leagues or countries.
- Helps challenge or refine an emerging interpretation.

## 2. Core positioning

**Sports Intelligence is a community for exploring what sports reveal about culture, business, society, tourism and technology.**

It is not a fan page, a prediction channel or a coding-only open-source project. People can contribute through group chat and discussion; the project maintainer can organise useful contributions into research briefs and case studies.

## 4. Low-friction contribution prompts

Use one question at a time. Avoid asking newcomers to complete a long template.

- What makes you choose to attend a live sports event rather than watch it at home?
- Which stadium or league has the most distinctive fan experience, and why?
- Have you ever travelled mainly to attend a sports event?
- What is one sports-business model you find interesting?
- What is one common assumption about sports fans that you think is wrong?

## 5. Two-week organic launch cadence

| Timing | Action | Desired signal |
|---|---|---|
| Day 1 | Share the Chinese invitation in the existing group | Replies and expressions of interest |
| Day 3 | Ask one focused KBO question; respond to every substantive reply | Conversation depth |
| Day 5 | Publish a short synthesis of the first observations, with contributors' permission | Evidence of follow-through |
| Day 7 | Invite people to add a counterargument or useful source | Repeat participation |
| Day 10 | Share one comparison question (KBO vs NPB / MLB) | New perspectives |
| Day 14 | Publish a concise community research note and invite the next topic | Returning contributors |

Do not post repeatedly without adding something new. Each public post should offer an observation, question, comparison or useful finding.

## 6. Acquisition metrics

Track weekly:
- People reached (only where the platform provides a reliable figure).
- People who replied or expressed interest.
- First-time contributors.
- Contributors who returned for a second interaction.
- Useful sources or first-hand examples received.
- Discussions that developed into a documented output.

The most important early metric is **returning contributors**, not GitHub stars.

## 7. Community response principles

- Respond personally and constructively to each meaningful contribution.
- Ask follow-up questions that deepen the topic rather than merely saying thanks.
- Distinguish personal experience from evidence-based claims.
- Ask permission before attributing or publishing someone's contribution outside the original group.
- Do not imply that a casual discussion is formal academic research.
- Never invent member counts, endorsements, partnerships or research findings.

## 8. Next growth milestone

After 10 active contributors have participated, review which topics generated the most useful discussion. Choose the next topic based on demonstrated interest, not assumptions. Then turn the strongest discussion into a public, well-sourced research brief that gives new visitors a reason to join.
